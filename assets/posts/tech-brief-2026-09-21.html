The September 21 DJ The PC Dude Tech Brief covers Windows Remote Desktop fixes, Windows 11 support planning, rclone security, counterfeit software installers, AI-themed phishing, hardware economics, and Google Business Profile consistency.

Windows Remote Desktop troubleshooting: check the corrective update first

When RDP suddenly becomes unstable after patching, check the Windows build and update history before replacing network hardware or rebuilding the OS. Post-update failures can be real regressions, and the corrective update may already exist.

Windows 11 support planning remains urgent

Run winver on every Windows machine. For systems approaching the end of their support window, verify backups, BitLocker recovery information, update health, Secure Boot/TPM status, and the safest supported upgrade path.

Linux: rclone and cloud-sync tools deserve security attention

Systems using rclone for cloud backups, NAS automation, or scheduled syncs should keep it patched and avoid exposing remote-control interfaces unnecessarily. Linux repair work should include service exposure and automation checks, not just desktop updates.

Counterfeit installers and AI-themed phishing are growing

Attackers continue to clone trusted software pages and use AI brand names to push fake installers, credential theft, remote-access tools, and information stealers.

Ask malware customers whether they recently installed an unofficial AI app, browser extension, plugin, cleaner, driver updater, or remote-support program.

Google Business Profile data is operational infrastructure

For local service businesses, stable business name, hours, services, website, service area, photos, and categories are increasingly important across Google’s local ecosystem. Audit them carefully, then avoid unnecessary churn.

Repair versus replacement still favors diagnosis

Before recommending a new computer, compare storage health, memory, thermals, malware, OS support, and the cost of a targeted repair. Known-good used components can also make older systems economically viable.

DJ The PC Dude takeaway

Good diagnostics come before replacement. Check the OS build, update history, storage health, memory, temperatures, drivers, malware indicators, backup status, and the real cost of repair versus replacement before spending money.
